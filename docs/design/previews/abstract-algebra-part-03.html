Groups — putting the properties together.

Review draft. Read the lesson, explore the example, and try the exercise before we generate the blog page.

Part 1 introduced closure. Part 2 introduced associativity, identity, and inverses. Now we combine these properties into one definition and see what it lets us prove.

By the end: recognize a group, explain why a near miss fails, and prove cancellation using the group properties.

A group is one kind of algebraic structure. Its requirements determine when the group label and its theorems apply. Operations that fail some requirements can still be studied.

The group definition

Definition — Group. A group is a set GGG with a binary operation ∗:G×G→G\ast:G\times G\to G∗:G×G→G satisfying:

  1. Associativity: (a∗b)∗c=a∗(b∗c)(a\ast b)\ast c=a\ast(b\ast c)(a∗b)∗c=a∗(b∗c) for all a,b,c∈Ga,b,c\in Ga,b,c∈G.
  2. Identity: there is an e∈Ge\in Ge∈G with e∗a=a∗e=ae\ast a=a\ast e=ae∗a=a∗e=a for every a∈Ga\in Ga∈G.
  3. Inverses: for every a∈Ga\in Ga∈G, there is an element a−1∈Ga^{-1}\in Ga−1∈G with a∗a−1=a−1∗a=ea\ast a^{-1}=a^{-1}\ast a=ea∗a−1=a−1∗a=e.

The notation a−1a^{-1}a−1 means the inverse for the chosen operation. For addition, this is −a-a−a; for multiplication of nonzero numbers, it is 1/a1/a1/a. The identity eee likewise depends on the operation.

Closure is included in the requirement that the operation maps G×GG\times GG×G into GGG. Check it explicitly when examining an example.

Commutativity is an additional property, not a group requirement. A group is called abelian when a∗b=b∗aa\ast b=b\ast aa∗b=b∗a for every pair of elements.

First example: even integers under addition

Let

E=2Z={…,−4,−2,0,2,4,…}.E=2\mathbb{Z}=\{\ldots,-4,-2,0,2,4,\ldots\}.E=2Z={…,−4,−2,0,2,4,…}.

Check each requirement:

  • Closure: if a=2ma=2ma=2m and b=2nb=2nb=2n, then a+b=2(m+n)a+b=2(m+n)a+b=2(m+n) is even.
  • Associativity: addition of even integers inherits associativity from integer addition.
  • Identity: 000 is even and 0+a=a+0=a0+a=a+0=a0+a=a+0=a.
  • Inverses: if a=2ma=2ma=2m, then −a=−2m=2(−m)-a=-2m=2(-m)−a=−2m=2(−m) is also even, and a+(−a)=0a+(-a)=0a+(−a)=0.

Therefore (E,+)(E,+)(E,+) is a group. It is also abelian because integer addition is commutative.

The inverse must stay inside the chosen set; finding an undoing element somewhere else is not enough.

A near miss: nonnegative integers under addition

For Z≥0={0,1,2,…}\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}Z≥0​={0,1,2,…}, addition is closed and associative, and 000 is an identity. But 111 has no additive inverse in this set: −1-1−1 is not nonnegative.

Thus (Z≥0,+)(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0},+)(Z≥0​,+) is not a group. It still has useful algebraic structure; it simply fails this definition.

Groups can consist of actions

Consider the rotations that carry a square onto itself. Write

G={e,r,r2,r3},G=\{e,r,r^2,r^3\},G={e,r,r2,r3},

where eee means no rotation and rrr means a counterclockwise quarter-turn. The operation combines rotations by composition. Powers represent repeated composition: r2r^2r2 is a 180∘180^\circ180∘ rotation, and r3r^3r3 is a 270∘270^\circ270∘ rotation. Four quarter-turns give the identity:

r4=e.r^4=e.r4=e.

A half-turn followed by a quarter-turn gives a three-quarter-turn. Four quarter-turns return the square to its starting position.

  • Closure: combining allowed rotations gives another allowed rotation.
  • Associativity: composition of these transformations is associative; regrouping three successive rotations does not change the final action.
  • Identity: eee changes nothing.
  • Inverses: eee is its own inverse, rrr and r3r^3r3 undo each other, and r2r^2r2 is its own inverse.

So these rotations form a group. Its elements are transformations of a square, rather than ordinary numbers. The same definition applies to both examples.

What do the requirements let us prove?

Proposition — Left cancellation. In a group, if a∗b=a∗ca\ast b=a\ast ca∗b=a∗c, then b=cb=cb=c.

Proof. Combine both sides on the left with the inverse of aaa:

a−1∗(a∗b)=a−1∗(a∗c).a^{-1}\ast(a\ast b)=a^{-1}\ast(a\ast c).a−1∗(a∗b)=a−1∗(a∗c).

Use associativity to regroup, keeping the order unchanged:

(a−1∗a)∗b=(a−1∗a)∗c.(a^{-1}\ast a)\ast b=(a^{-1}\ast a)\ast c.(a−1∗a)∗b=(a−1∗a)∗c.

The inverse and identity properties give

e∗b=e∗c,e\ast b=e\ast c,e∗b=e∗c,

hence b=cb=cb=c. □\square□

We used an inverse to undo aaa, associativity to move the parentheses, and the identity to simplify the result. We did not use commutativity.

Cancellation is not automatically valid for every operation. Under integer multiplication, 0⋅2=0⋅30\cdot2=0\cdot30⋅2=0⋅3, while 2≠32\ne32=3. The group proof cannot be applied: 000 has no multiplicative inverse.
